I had run Hertape for three years before we did our first pop-up. I thought I was ready. I had the products, the team, the aesthetics locked. What I didn't have was any real understanding of what it means to operate in physical space — and how differently people behave when they can touch things.

Here's what I wish someone had told me before we opened the doors.

1. **The layout is the product.** How you arrange a physical space shapes what people buy and how long they stay. We redesigned our floor layout three times across three pop-ups. Every change moved the numbers. It's not decoration — it's conversion rate optimization in three dimensions.

2. **Your best customers will show up on day one.** The people who follow you most closely, who've been waiting — they come first. Build something worth their loyalty. The energy of that first wave sets the tone for everything that follows.

3. **Offline traffic doesn't convert online automatically.** We had thousands of people walk through. Our online sales barely moved during the event. The bridge between offline and online has to be built deliberately — QR codes, exclusive drops, email capture at the door. It doesn't happen by itself.

4. **Your team's energy is visible.** When my team was tired, customers felt it. When they were excited, the space felt different. Managing morale during a 9-day run is as important as any logistics decision.

5. **The real value comes after.** The best thing that came from our pop-ups wasn't the revenue during the event. It was the content, the community, the trust signals for customers who weren't there. Document everything. The pop-up ends. The content doesn't.
