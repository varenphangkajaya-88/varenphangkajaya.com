2025

Five lessons no one taught me until I opened my own pop-up

I had run Hertape for three years before we did our first pop-up. I thought I was ready. I had the products, the team, the aesthetics locked. What I didn't have was any real understanding of what it means to operate in physical space — and how differently people behave when they can touch things.

Here's what I wish someone had told me before we opened the doors.

  1. The layout is the product. How you arrange a physical space shapes what people buy and how long they stay. We redesigned our floor layout three times across three pop-ups. Every change moved the numbers. It's not decoration — it's conversion rate optimization in three dimensions.
  2. Your best customers will show up on day one. The people who follow you most closely, who've been waiting — they come first. Build something worth their loyalty. The energy of that first wave sets the tone for everything that follows.
Photo
Photo
  1. Offline traffic doesn't convert online automatically. We had thousands of people walk through. Our online sales barely moved during the event. The bridge between offline and online has to be built deliberately — QR codes, exclusive drops, email capture at the door. It doesn't happen by itself.
  2. Your team's energy is visible. When my team was tired, customers felt it. When they were excited, the space felt different. Managing morale during a 9-day run is as important as any logistics decision.
  3. The real value comes after. The best thing that came from our pop-ups wasn't the revenue during the event. It was the content, the community, the trust signals for customers who weren't there. Document everything. The pop-up ends. The content doesn't.
Hero photo
2025

If I could start my business again, I'd do these five things differently

Hertape didn't start with a plan. It started with a product I wanted to exist and a stubborn belief that I could figure out the rest as I went. That worked — until it didn't. Here's the honest version of what I'd change.

  1. I'd build the system before I needed it. I spent years running operations in my head and on WhatsApp. By the time I built Hertape Smart App — a proper internal OS for the team — we were already behind. Systems should be infrastructure, not a rescue mission.
  2. I'd collect emails from day one. We had customers before we had a list. Every sale that happened before we had CRM set up was a relationship we couldn't build on. Email is the only channel you actually own.
Photo
Photo
  1. I'd charge more, earlier. Underpricing is a confidence problem wearing a strategy costume. The customers who push back on price are rarely the ones who become your best customers anyway.
  2. I'd document the brand before building the product. We had a clear aesthetic in our heads for years before we wrote it down. That meant every new team member had to reverse-engineer the vibe from existing work. A proper brand guide would have saved us months.
  3. I'd build in public. The brands that grew fastest around us weren't necessarily the best — they were the most visible about their process. People don't just buy products. They buy into stories. I started too late.

None of this is regret. Hertape is what it is because of everything I learned the hard way. But if you're earlier in the journey than I was — maybe some of this saves you a lap.
