A few weeks ago, Hertape opened its first pop-up — and then a second one, in another city, almost back to back. It was the most ambitious thing I've built so far. And somewhere between the build-up, the breakdown, and the buzz of opening day, I learned five things no founder course could have prepared me for.

1. **You'll never be fully prepared.** Even with double the time and double the team, the unexpected still found a way in. Power issues, AC leaks, floods, traffic, the wrong carpet color. I used to spiral and tell myself I should have planned better. But I've learned — not everything is predictable. Focus on what you can control. Some flaws don't break you. They sharpen you.
2. **The team is everything.** Choosing your team is like choosing your front line. They are the first energy people feel when they walk into your space. Their passion, empathy, problem-solving, and creativity — all of it becomes a reflection of you. They don't just work for the brand. They are the brand in motion. Hire accordingly, and choose people who genuinely believe in what you're building.
3. **You can't please everyone.** No matter how much heart you pour in, there will always be complaints — slow replies, incomplete stock, sold-out pieces. I used to take every bad review personally and overthink for days. But I've learned: people come with different expectations, and you can't make everyone happy. Take the constructive feedback. Ignore the unnecessary weight. You grow faster the moment you stop trying to please everyone.
4. **Curation sets the standard.** From tenants, vendors, and F&B placement, to team grooming rules, interior finishes, and influencer lists — every detail was intentionally curated. Including who you let in to vibe with you. Not to be perfectionist or overly idealistic, but because without strong curation, your direction gets lost and your brand energy gets diluted. First impressions live forever in your audience's mind — good or bad.
5. **Being a founder isn't supposed to be easy.** A few days before the event, the doubts got loud. What if it fails? What if people are disappointed? Was I too greedy expanding into two cities at once? But then I remembered — this path was never meant to be easy or comfortable. Being a founder isn't just about starting something. It's about continuously discovering — the business, the problems, and most importantly… yourself.

If you're building something of your own right now, I hope this reaches you on the right day. Keep going. — Varen
