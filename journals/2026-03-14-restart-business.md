Hindsight is a strange gift. You can't undo the years, but you can name what you wish you'd known. After running Hertape for a few years now — through pop-ups, marketplaces, scaling, and rebuilding — these are the five things I'd do differently if I had to start again from day one.

1. **I'd start earlier, instead of looking for the "right formula."** When I first started, I thought successful founders had some kind of guidebook. Like there was a formula you needed to figure out before you began. The truth is — there isn't. Most founders just start before they feel ready. In reality, the mistakes and failures you go through become your guidebook.

2. **I'd leave marketplaces much sooner.** Marketplaces are great for helping a brand start. But they're not designed to help you build a brand long-term. You don't own the customer relationship. You don't control the brand experience. And you don't really understand your customers. That's one of the reasons Hertape eventually left all marketplaces. (I'll share more about that in another post.)

3. **I'd own my customer data from day one.** For the first few years, I knew almost nothing about my customers. No behavior data, no real relationship. How am I supposed to invite customers to our pop-up store if all I have are censored names and phone numbers? It felt like running a store but never knowing who actually walked in.

4. **I'd stop obsessing over new customers.** In the beginning, I spent most of my time chasing new customers. Ninety percent acquiring, ten percent retaining. If I could start again, I'd flip that completely. Sixty percent retaining customers, forty percent acquiring new ones. You'd be surprised how powerful customer retention actually is.

5. **I wouldn't rush into scaling.** At the beginning, I thought a "real business" meant having an office and a big team. So we did that. But looking back, we scaled the cost before we scaled the business. Today, Hertape has no office and only eight team members — none of them full-time. And somehow, with the right systems in place, we're more efficient than before. Now I see it differently: first make the business work. Then expand.

If you're earlier in the journey than I was, take this as a quiet shortcut. None of these lessons are glamorous, but every one of them would have saved me a year.

Keep building. — Varen
